# Interview Notes — raw material for the book

*These are Missy's own words from our conversations, kept here so nothing gets
lost. The polished introduction in `book.json` is built from these.*

## The basics
- Name: Missy Delaria
- Met husband in 1989; college sweethearts
- Married 1995 in Wayzata, Minnesota
- Bought a little house in Deep Haven, Minnesota
- Kendall born August 1997 (first child)
- Twins Stockton and Larkin born September 1999
- Family of five
- Chose to stay home; devoted 28 years to raising the kids and feeding the family

## Where she grew up & how she learned to cook
- Grew up in Grand Island, a suburb outside Buffalo, New York
- Home was on the Niagara River, overlooking Canada — this is where she learned to cook
- Childhood home: typical family of four; mother stayed home, father worked
- Has a sister six and a half years older, who was off doing other things — so Missy
  largely learned to cook on her own
- Her mom and grandmother were not big cooks, but she still has a couple of their
  recipes that she uses to this day
- Learned her basic cooking technique from **Suzie — Mrs. Wiles**, a housekeeper in the neighborhood
- Says she really learned to cook from three sources: Mrs. Wiles (the foundation),
  a little from her mom, and a lot from herself — experimenting, trying, and having
  the drive and desire to cook good food for family and friends
- Meals changed as her parents got older and the girls were in high school

## Her cooking style / philosophy
- Region isn't strictly defined: American, European, a little Southern
- Her cooking is defined by *taste* and by how much her family and friends enjoy it —
  that's the only measure that matters to her

## The family table (a core philosophy)
- Cooking started from the desire to have the whole family together at the table
  every night — even when the kids were babies
- One family meal — never cooked multiple/separate meals. "The family food was the
  family food, and the kids ate everything." Nothing made special for the kids.
- Kids had favorites, but learned to take a "no thank you helping"
- Cooked balanced and nutritious so the kids got all aspects to feed growing bodies
- Kids served themselves once able, carried plates to their place, and waited until
  Mom and Dad were served so everyone sat down together — no rushing through dinner
- Nightly ritual: everyone shares one good thing and one bad thing from their day
  - good things are celebrated by the people you love
  - bad things can be solved or comforted by the people you love
- Kept the family-dinner tradition all the way through high school, even when the
  kids were very active
