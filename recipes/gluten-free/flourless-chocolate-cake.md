## Story
When my niece was diagnosed with celiac, the thing she cried about wasn't bread — it was that she thought she'd never have a "real" birthday cake again. So I went looking, and I learned the most freeing lesson in gluten-free baking: the best GF desserts often aren't substitutes at all. This cake never had flour to begin with. She blew out the candles, took one bite, and said "this is better." It is. This is the first of my gluten-free teachings: start with what's naturally gluten-free before you reach for a swap.

## Ingredients
- 8 oz good dark chocolate (70%), chopped
- 1 cup (2 sticks) unsalted butter
- 1 1/4 cups granulated sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon vanilla extract
- 6 large eggs
- 1 cup unsweetened cocoa powder, plus more for dusting

## Steps
1. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Butter a 9-inch springform pan and line the bottom with parchment.
2. Melt the chocolate and butter together gently — a double boiler or short microwave bursts — and stir smooth.
3. Whisk in the sugar, salt, and vanilla. Let it cool for a couple of minutes so it doesn't cook the eggs.
4. Whisk in the eggs one at a time until the batter is glossy, then sift in the cocoa and fold just until combined.
5. Pour into the pan and bake 35 to 40 minutes, until the top is set with a thin crust and the center is just barely firm.
6. Cool completely in the pan, then dust with cocoa. It's even better the next day.

## Make It Your Own
- A teaspoon of espresso powder deepens the chocolate without tasting like coffee.
- Add the grated zest of an orange, or a pinch of cinnamon and cayenne for a Mexican-chocolate version.
- Serve with whipped cream and raspberries, or a scoop of ice cream for the kids.
- Naturally gluten-free already — just confirm your cocoa and chocolate are certified GF if cross-contamination is a concern.

## Notes
A clean knife wiped between slices gives those bakery-perfect wedges. Warm the knife under hot water for the cleanest cut.
