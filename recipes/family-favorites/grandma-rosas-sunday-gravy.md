## Story
My great-grandmother Rosa came over with this in her head, never on paper. "Gravy," in our family, never meant the brown stuff — it meant a pot of tomato sauce simmering with meat from before church until after. The whole house smelled like Sunday. The rule she gave my mother, who gave it to me, was this: never rush the gravy, and always taste it with bread you've torn, not cut. I have no idea why the bread has to be torn. I do it anyway.

## Ingredients
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 lb pork spare ribs or a pork shoulder chunk
- 6 Italian sausages
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 2 (28 oz) cans whole peeled tomatoes, crushed by hand
- 1 cup water (swirl it in the cans to get every bit)
- 2 teaspoons salt, plus more to taste
- A small handful of fresh basil
- A pinch of sugar, only if the tomatoes are sharp

## Steps
1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot. Brown the pork and sausage on all sides, then set them aside. Don't crowd the pot.
2. Lower the heat. Cook the onion in the drippings until soft and golden, about 8 minutes, then add the garlic for one more minute.
3. Stir in the tomato paste and let it darken for a minute or two — this is where the deep flavor hides.
4. Add the crushed tomatoes, the swirl water, and the salt. Return the meat to the pot.
5. Bring to a bare bubble, then drop to the lowest simmer. Partly cover and cook 3 to 4 hours, stirring now and then so the bottom doesn't catch.
6. Pull the meat out, tear the basil in, taste, and adjust salt (and that pinch of sugar if needed). Serve the sauce over pasta and the meat on a platter alongside.

## Make It Your Own
- No pork? Use beef short ribs, meatballs, or skip the meat entirely and add mushrooms for body.
- Want it weeknight-fast? Brown just the sausage, skip the long braise, and simmer 45 minutes.
- We like it smooth; my sister likes it chunky. Blend half, or none, to your taste.
- A parmesan rind dropped in while it simmers adds a savory depth that's hard to name and easy to love.

## Notes
This freezes beautifully. I make a double batch and freeze flat in bags so a Sunday dinner is never more than a reheat away.
